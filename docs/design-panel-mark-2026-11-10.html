0. What the research says

Four questions: how the pore is drawn by the people who study it; what makes a ring mark hold up at 16 px; what a favicon has to survive; and how cargo and gate are shown. Sources are linked where they are used and listed at the end.

0.1 The pore, as structural biology draws it

  • Top view: a ring of eight round a wide channel. “NPCs form an extensive 120 nm-wide protein scaffold of three stacked rings … Each ring comprises eight spokes that surround a 40 to 50 nm-wide transport channel” (Mosalaganti et al., bioRxiv 2021 (Science 2022)). In intact human cells the channel opens to 57 nm (GEN on Schuller et al., Nature 2021). Channel to outer width: 0.33 to 0.48.
  • The rings come in eights and in pairs. Coat complexes “associate to form four eight-membered rings” (PDB-101, Nuclear Pore Complex (Goodsell, Markosian, 2017)): a cytoplasmic, a nuclear and an inner ring, stacked (Mosalaganti et al., bioRxiv 2021 (Science 2022)). EMBL calls the whole “a doughnut-shaped molecular complex” of 120 MDa (EMBL news, 10 June 2022).
  • The spokes reach into the channel, and the channel is full. The eight subunits “project a spoke-shaped protein over the pore channel” (Wikipedia, Nuclear pore); “a tangle of unstructured chains that fill the central pore” (PDB-101, Nuclear Pore Complex (Goodsell, Markosian, 2017)); Basel’s 2025 picture is “a ring-shaped pore with thread-like extensions reaching into the centre”, where transport factors “loosely form a central plug” (University of Basel, “Shapeshifting gates guard the cell nucleus”, 3 Dec 2025). M1’s dot is defensible as that plug; drawn as a dot, it reads as a target.
  • The side view belongs to covers and textbooks. Science’s cover of 10 June 2022 is “the cross-sectional view of the human nuclear pore complex”; the pore “pierces the double membrane” (SBGrid, “The monstrous maw”, on the Science cover of 10 June 2022). Wikipedia’s schematic labels outer ring, spokes, basket and filaments (Wikipedia, Nuclear pore). The basket and filaments are the asymmetric parts and are too fine for a mark.
  • What says “pore” at a glance: a thick ring of eight round an open channel, seen from above. The double membrane and the basket need a side view and a caption.

  • Each arrangement brings a reading. Teeth on the outside say gear (Rust). Spokes to a hub say wheel or helm: Kubernetes has seven sides as “an homage” to Project Seven of Nine (McLuckie, “From Google to the world: the Kubernetes origin story”, 2016), and an eight-spoked wheel is the dharma wheel, ☸ U+2638 (Wikipedia, Dharmachakra). A ring of bars says spinner: an activity indicator is “a circular image that appears to spin” (Apple HIG, Progress indicators). A ring with a dot is Target’s.
  • From the list in the brief, these are not ring or spoke marks: Octopus Deploy (an octopus), Tor (an onion), Drupal (a drop), Podman (seals in a badge), Mozilla (the wordmark). Docker’s mark in Simple Icons is still the whale. Ring and Radiolab were not checked (section 3).

0.3 What a favicon has to survive

  • Few shapes, no hairlines. “Embrace simplicity in your icon design”, “with a minimal number of shapes”; “avoid extremely thin line weights and sharp corners, because they tend to lose detail and crispness in smaller icon sizes” (Apple HIG, App icons).
  • A separate drawing for small sizes is normal. If the 16 px scale-down blurs, make “a special version of the logo that’s tailored to fit small pixel grids” (Evil Martians, “How to Favicon in 2026”). Material Symbols do it by axis: “the stroke weight (thickness) changes as the icon size scales”, 20 to 48 dp (Google, Material Symbols guide).
  • The pixel grid has four-fold symmetry, not eight. Only quarter turns and mirror lines map the 16×16 grid onto itself. Eight elements at 22.5° + k·45° are mirror images of each other and rasterise as one shape; at 0° + k·45°, as M1 is, they come in two kinds. An octagon’s 45° edges are exact one-pixel stairs. (This panel’s analysis; figure 1.)
  • Light and dark tab bars. A prefers-color-scheme query inside the SVG switches the colour (web.dev, Building an adaptive favicon); it follows the system or browser theme, not the page. SVG favicons work in Chrome, Firefox and, since version 26, Safari (Can I use, SVG favicons). The site is dark only, but its tab sits in a light bar on a light system: hence #4a3d9a there, as favicon.svg does now.
  • Touch icons are opaque and masked by the system. iOS “applies masking to produce rounded corners”; “Keep primary content centered” (Apple HIG, App icons). 180×180 px with about 20 px of padding and a background colour (Evil Martians, “How to Favicon in 2026”). Maskable icons keep content within a circle of radius 40 % of the width (web.dev, Adaptive icon support in PWAs with maskable icons); the site has no web manifest, so that does not apply yet.

0.4 Gate and cargo in science communication

  • The nuclear localisation signal is “a special tag”, and importin-β “is folded like a spring, which then wraps into a big spiral that traps its binding partners inside”; the pores are “the highway connecting the nucleus and the cytoplasm” (PDB-101, Importins (Goodsell, 2007)).
  • Basel’s 2025 release: “Imagine the cell’s nucleus as a bank vault”; “Only proteins carrying the correct ‘key’ … are granted exclusive access”. Its animation leaves the cargo out “for clarity” (University of Basel, “Shapeshifting gates guard the cell nucleus”, 3 Dec 2025).
  • For the mark: the hero already tells the cargo story (letter, address tag, importin’s cup). Told again at 16 px, a letter gets 6×4 px (K5).

0.5 What follows for the mark

  • An open channel, about half the width (pore 0.33–0.48, hero 0.55), and no dot.
  • At 16 px, eight separate subunits are dashes or rays, never a pore (figure 1). The 16 px form is therefore a closed shape that keeps the eight in its outline; the subunits show from 24 px up.
  • Elements on the 22.5° positions, or an outline whose diagonals are 45°, so that the pixel form has one kind of subunit.
  • No outer teeth, no spokes to a hub, no rays round a dot, no ring with a dot: gear, wheel, sun or spinner, target.
  • One colour and still, as M1’s rule has it: the mark never turns.

2. Recommendation and build notes

Recommendation, ranked
  1. K7, the octagonal ring of eight facets. At 16 px it is the cleanest closed ring of the set: no grey pixel, 136 of 256 pixels on (M1: 52), the channel open, so it holds a tab as Target and CircleCI do. The eight is left there only as the octagon’s sides, which is more than the dashes or rays of the others keep. From 24 px up the eight facets show, one at 12 o’clock, on the radii of the hero’s subunits (figure 3). No dot, no rays, no teeth, no hub. Its risk: an octagon can recall a stop sign or a nut. The colour and the open channel argue against both, but that is judgement, not a test.
  2. K1, tapered wedges, if a round outline matters more than the eight at 16 px. It is the closest to the hero’s round pore. Its own 16 px form is a dashed circle (figure 1), so it should borrow K7’s octagonal ring for the favicon: at 16 px a round ring of that thickness is an octagon anyway.
  3. K2, the double ring. The most literal reading of the structure, two eight-membered rings, and handsome at 128. At 32 px its 2-px rings are at the limit, and at 16 px it is a dashed ring round a ring.

Not recommended: K3 and M1 read as spinners or a target; K4 needs a caption; K6 is a gear; K8 is a wheel from 32 px up. K5’s letter belongs at large sizes only, if anywhere.

3. What I could not verify

  • The pictures themselves. I read the text and captions of the EMBL, PDB-101, SBGrid and Basel pages, not the images; the Science cover is known here from SBGrid’s description.
  • The PMC copies of the structure reviews answered with a captcha. Dimensions come from the Mosalaganti preprint (read in full text) and from GEN’s report of Schuller et al. (57 nm); I did not read the Schuller paper.
  • Material’s system-icon grid page did not render (it needs JavaScript), so its keylines are not used. Apple’s HIG text came from the HIG’s JSON; its grid templates are design files I did not open. The Icons8 article on tiny icons answered 403.
  • The brand marks were measured from Simple Icons’ outlines, not from the brands’ own favicons, some of which may be drawn separately for small sizes.
  • A “whale-free” Docker mark, Radiolab’s dot ring, Ring’s mark and Mozilla’s older ring marks from the brief: not found or not checked.
  • How Chrome and Safari rasterise an SVG favicon on a 2× screen. The panel assumes the 16 px form is drawn doubled, as figure 1 shows. web.dev names Firefox and Chromium for media queries inside SVG favicons; Safari’s handling of prefers-color-scheme there is untested.
  • Whether readers see a stop sign or a nut in K7, or a spinner in K1: judgement, not tested with people.
